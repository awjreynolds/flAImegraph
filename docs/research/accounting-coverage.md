# Existing cost and work-allocation semantics for AI profiling

Research date: 2026-09-06. Scope: the accounting side of an interoperable AI usage profile, especially attribution from model calls through tickets to epics. Primary baseline: **FOCUS publication version 1.4**; development proposals are identified separately. This is a research resolution, not approval of an architecture or a new standard.

## Finding

FOCUS already supplies much of the financial vocabulary and conservation logic this effort needs. It is a billing-data specification, rather than a model-call trace format. The plausible contribution is a tested correspondence between execution evidence, financial records, and work attribution—not a replacement definition of tokens, billed cost, or shared allocation. This is an inference from the reviewed specifications, not a claim that no other project covers that correspondence.

The version boundary matters. The [published FOCUS 1.4 specification](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/) identifies itself as released. The official [1.5 release scope](https://focus.finops.org/focus-1-5-release-scope/) identifies 1.4 as current and 1.5 as development toward December 2026. A merged working-draft contribution is therefore not yet released coverage. Provider adoption may also lag: a generator exporting an earlier version does not inherit newer requirements merely because the specification exists.

## What the accounting terms actually commit us to

**Billed cost is financial evidence.** FOCUS 1.4 explicitly excludes estimated or inferred values from `BilledCost`. It requires zero billed cost from entities lacking responsibility or authorization to invoice, preventing duplicate billing when datasets are combined. Issued-invoice totals must reconcile within a specified rounding tolerance. Consequently, a local profiler should retain estimates as estimates and import actual billing evidence separately; assigning estimated dollars to calls does not establish that those dollars were invoiced. [Billed Cost requirements](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/billed-cost/)

**Effective cost is recognition, not a synonym for “our best estimate.”** Covered usage incorporates recognized portions of purchase costs, while purchases intended to cover related usage have zero effective cost. The specification constrains totals across related covering and covered charges when both are present. A monthly effective-cost total can differ from billed cost when the relationship spans periods/accounts or one side is absent. This explains why summing a ticket's per-call list-price calculations may disagree legitimately with its allocated financial cost. [Effective Cost requirements](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/effective-cost/)

List and contracted costs answer separate pricing questions. Both specifications warn against counting covering purchases and their covered usage together in savings calculations. A proposed flame graph must therefore identify its selected cost basis and aggregation scope. “Cost” without a basis is insufficient for comparisons between tickets or implementations. [List Cost aggregation](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/list-cost/), [Contracted Cost aggregation](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/contracted-cost/)

FOCUS also already addresses subscriptions and virtual-currency-style arrangements through its broader purchase and usage model. A subscription fee is not evidence that any particular call incurred the equivalent public API price. The [FinOps Foundation's AI tracker guidance](https://www.finops.org/wg/how-to-build-a-generative-ai-cost-and-usage-tracker/) discusses request-level token capture, use-case attribution, model-price history, and shared provisioned capacity. This is useful prior art and implementation guidance, not a normative exchange format. Its recognition that fixed capacity still generates tokens without being billed per token is particularly relevant; its illustrative prices and calculations should not be adopted as current universal billing rules.

## Existing allocation versus unresolved policy

The released split-allocation mechanism is substantial. Allocated records preserve original dimension values and non-summable metrics such as unit prices; their summable metrics must reconstruct the original charge. [Allocation handling](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/attributes/data-generator-calculated-split-cost-allocation-handling/) `AllocatedMethodDetails` specifies ratios totaling one across the applicable allocations and can document measured factors and custom properties. [Method details](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/allocated-method-details/)

This separates *recording an allocation* from *selecting an allocation rule*. Charging a shared research run to its initiating ticket, splitting it among beneficiaries, or keeping it as epic overhead are different valid policies. An interoperability profile could require a policy identifier and evidence references without prescribing one universally correct economic allocation. An unallocated bucket is preferable to inventing certainty.

`Tags` are finalized key/value context, with generator-defined resolution behavior when multiple levels provide competing values. `AllocatedTags` specifically records allocation-related context and excludes resource tags already present in `Tags`. [Tag semantics](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/tags/), [Allocated Tags](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/allocated-tags/) A ticket identifier can fit as business context, but the standard does not thereby decide whether epic membership is evaluated at execution time, closure time, or report time. That is a versioned application-policy decision, not missing financial arithmetic.

## Active standardization already overlaps this idea

Four development areas should be followed before proposing fields:

1. **Model identity:** [AI model identity as SkuPriceDetails properties](https://github.com/FinOps-Open-Cost-and-Usage-Spec/FOCUS_Spec/pull/2442) merged into `working_draft` on July 20, 2026. It introduces model developer, family, ID, and version properties. Its input/output distinction uses separate SKUs/price IDs and `SkuMeter`; it explicitly leaves per-request max-token configuration outside that billing scope. This is a valuable existing boundary between pricing semantics and observability.
2. **Cache pricing:** [Distinguish cached from uncached token costs](https://github.com/FinOps-Open-Cost-and-Usage-Spec/FOCUS_Spec/issues/2099) is open. It identifies normalization as the problem where underlying provider data exists. Its current scope defers cache-retention-duration normalization while retaining distinct price IDs for price points. The release-scope page lists cache pricing dimensions as a 1.5 stretch goal, not released capability.
3. **Actors:** [Add Actor dimensions to Cost and Usage](https://github.com/FinOps-Open-Cost-and-Usage-Spec/FOCUS_Spec/issues/2358) is open on the 1.5 milestone. The original principal/consumer proposal has evolved: its August 26 meeting update discusses requester details and credential identity. The [related credential-details proposal](https://github.com/FinOps-Open-Cost-and-Usage-Spec/FOCUS_Spec/pull/2553) distinguishes authentication information from an application's consumer construct. Indexed pages show different revision ages; proposed field names should be rechecked against the eventual release rather than copied from the original issue.
4. **Price catalog:** [Add SKU Price dataset](https://github.com/FinOps-Open-Cost-and-Usage-Spec/FOCUS_Spec/pull/2424) is open. It proposes independently published rates, validity periods, pricing tiers, eligibility, and links to usage by price ID. That directly overlaps any plan to standardize “what would this have cost?” inputs. The live [1.5 scope](https://focus.finops.org/focus-1-5-release-scope/) also includes AI billing examples and allocation work.

## Five test cases for any proposed bridge

These are synthetic acceptance scenarios, not claims about a particular provider's current prices.

**Cached input plus retries.** One logical operation makes two actual model requests. Each has its own input/output and cache evidence; one fails after consuming tokens. Count actual requests once, retain the logical-operation relationship, and map each billing component once. Double-counting cached tokens already included in a provider's input total is a source-normalization error. Missing usage from a disconnected response is missing evidence; a schema cannot recover the amount.

**One agent helps two tickets.** A $10 research run benefits two tickets in the same epic. Full $10 entries under both tickets incorrectly produce $20 at epic level. A chosen 60/40 split produces $6 and $4, while a causal execution view retains one $10 run. Attribution edges and execution-parent relationships serve different purposes. The conservation principle already exists in FOCUS; deciding 60/40 requires policy or evidence.

**Fixed monthly subscription.** A $200 seat is used for three tickets and general exploration. The invoice establishes $200, but tokens alone do not establish each ticket's marginal charge. Allocate the fee under a named policy and retain overhead/unallocated usage. An API-equivalent calculation is a counterfactual comparison; adding it to the subscription would invent expenditure.

**Late refund and revised export.** An invoiced $12 usage charge is later reduced by $2. Preserve the original execution's consumption evidence and the financial correction. With a replacement export, loading both snapshots as additive records is wrong; with delta/ledger delivery, ignoring the correction is wrong. A flame graph must also specify how negative financial adjustments appear: an ordinary positive-width cost tree cannot silently discard them. The delivery semantics are already covered; the visualization treatment needs a rule.

**Provisioned capacity and incomplete tracing.** An epic runs on shared capacity costing $1,000 for a period; only 70% of its requests were captured. Neither extrapolated token cost nor captured call totals prove complete financial attribution. Retain the $1,000 financial amount, known usage evidence, allocation method, and unresolved residual. Billing completeness and trace completeness are separate dimensions, even if a particular report uses both.

## Smallest plausible extension and contribution route

The strongest candidate is an **interoperability profile with worked fixtures**: existing execution identifiers and measurements on one side, existing FOCUS cost meanings on the other, with explicit many-to-many correspondence and allocation evidence between them. Candidate requirements are stable source/dataset references, execution/work-item references, measurement-versus-estimate provenance, allocation-policy identity, reconciliation scope, and uncovered residuals. These are research recommendations, not proposed normative field names. Use existing tags or documented custom extension points while testing; FOCUS requires `x_` prefixes for custom columns. [Custom Column Handling](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/attributes/custom-column-handling/)

The test of value is whether two independent implementations derive the same totals from identical call records, invoice data, and a declared allocation policy. “Cost per completed epic” additionally requires an agreed completion/outcome denominator; FinOps already treats business-performance data as an input to [unit economics](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/unit-economics/). That definition should not be smuggled into the meaning of a token.

Contribute concrete examples and demonstrated failures to the existing AI, allocation, and price-catalog work before asking for a new standard. FOCUS offers public issue feedback and community calls; formal contributing membership involves its agreement and CLA process. [FOCUS participation](https://focus.finops.org/feedback/) No external contact was made for this research. The unresolved bridge should next be tested against real, sanitized session and billing exports; absent evidence is an instrumentation/provider problem, while competing ways to assign shared costs are policy questions.
